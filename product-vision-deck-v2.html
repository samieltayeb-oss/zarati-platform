03 The Problem
Sudan Agriculture
~0%
of agricultural production is digitally tracked
70%
of Sudan's workforce depends on agriculture — yet the sector operates entirely on informal oral networks
الزراعة السودانية تعمل بدون بيانات
📊
No data, no decisions
Farmers lack price data, weather forecasts, and crop advisory. They make seasonal decisions worth their family's entire income on rumors and tradition.
📡
Government is flying blind
The Ministry of Agriculture has no real-time production data. Food security decisions, subsidy allocation, and export policy are based on estimates from the 1990s.
💸
Traders & middlemen extract value
Without market price visibility, farmers sell to whoever arrives at the farm gate. A study of similar markets finds smallholders capture 30–40% below fair market price.
🤲
Aid is inefficient and unverifiable
$600M+ in annual humanitarian agricultural aid reaches Sudan without beneficiary verification, impact tracking, or outcome reporting — damaging donor trust and renewal.
👩‍🌾
Women farmers are invisible
Women represent an estimated 60–80% of food production labor in Sudan but are largely excluded from registered services, credit access, and agricultural programs.

06 Governance & Political Reality
Honest Assessment
Sudan's governance landscape is fragmented — and that is our context
We do not hide political complexity. Our strategy accounts for it explicitly.
Gedaref, Kassala, Red Sea — Phase 1 (Active)
N. Kordofan, Nile State — Phase 2 (conditional)
Khartoum, Gezira — Excluded (conflict)
Fragmented political authority
The SAF-led government (Port Sudan) controls eastern states. ZARATI Phase 1 is geographically aligned with the recognized government's territory and agriculture ministry.
State-level relationships are primary
Agricultural decisions in Sudan are largely state-level. Gedaref, Kassala, and Red Sea state governments have operational authority. We engage there first.
Neutral platform positioning
ZARATI is a data and services platform, not a political actor. FAO, WFP, and GIZ already operate in these states under humanitarian mandates. We follow their engagement models.
Data sovereignty & government trust
All agricultural data has a clear sovereignty framework: farmer data owned by farmers, aggregate data shared with government via formal data-sharing agreements with clear governance.
Engagement Strategy
Step 1: Humanitarian entry
Launch under FAO/WFP pilot agreements — this gives us legitimacy, data, and political cover.
Step 2: State government MOU
Formal data-sharing MOU with state agriculture ministries before national expansion.
Step 3: Federal MOU for national rollout
National data platform agreement with Sudan Federal Ministry of Agriculture (Port Sudan).
Step 4: Post-conflict expansion
When Khartoum/Gezira stabilize, ZARATI is already the established national agricultural data standard.
KEY RISK MITIGATION
ZARATI does not require national political consensus to operate. Phase 1 is viable with state-level agreements alone.

08 Why Digital, Why Now
The enabling conditions
Three forces converging now
1. Mobile penetration reaching critical mass
68% rural mobile penetration. Basic-phone SMS capability means ZARATI works for farmers without smartphones or internet. Reaching 10M+ farmers is technically feasible today.
2. Humanitarian sector demanding accountability
Post-COVID, every major donor (USAID, DFID, EU) requires beneficiary tracking, impact verification, and outcome reporting. ZARATI solves a $600M problem NGOs currently cannot address.
3. Reconstruction creates a once-in-generation window
Post-conflict reconstruction investment in Sudan agriculture is projected at $3B+ (World Bank/IDA estimates). The platform that owns the data layer when reconstruction begins owns the sector.
Why not wait for stability?
"The data layer that captures the crisis period becomes the baseline. Whoever builds it first wins."
M-Pesa launched during Kenya's 2007–08 post-election crisis. Grameen Bank scaled fastest during Bangladesh floods. Crisis creates demand for trust infrastructure.
First-mover in data accumulation creates compounding advantages: better AI, better pricing, better government relationships, stronger network effects.

17 SMS & Offline-First
Infrastructure reality
A platform built for no internet, no smartphone, no power
Most AgriTech platforms fail in Sudan because they assume internet access. ZARATI does not. SMS is the baseline — everything else is an enhancement.
📱 SMS / USSD
Market prices, weather, advisory, registration — all via basic SMS commands. Works on any handset. No app install needed.
📶 Progressive web app
For smartphones: full PWA with offline caching. Works without internet after initial load. Syncs when connection available.
🤝 Community agents
Trained local intermediaries serve farmers with no phone access. Batch data entry via agent app. Proven model in East Africa.
🔁 Async sync
All data operations designed as async. Queue locally, sync when connected. No data loss from interruptions.

18 Satellite Monitoring
Remote sensing capability
See every field without visiting it
Using openly available satellite data (ESA Sentinel-2, NASA MODIS, CHIRPS), ZARATI monitors registered farmland without requiring on-ground access — critical in conflict-affected areas.
NDVI crop health monitoring
Normalized Difference Vegetation Index — tracks crop biomass and health over growing season. Identifies stress zones before visible.
Flood and drought detection
Radar imagery detects flood extent within 24–48 hours. Combined with farm registry, generates automatic affected-farmer lists for NGO response.
Crop type and area estimation
Machine learning classification of crop types from satellite imagery — enables production estimates where ground surveys are impossible.

## 1. Executive Summary

Zarati is architected as the premier **Sovereign Agricultural Intelligence Operating System (Agri-OS)** for Sudan and emerging markets. Addressing an agricultural sector that sustains 65% to 80% of Sudan's population and represents 175 million feddans of arable land, Zarati bridges the fatal gap between macro-level food security governance and micro-level smallholder transaction reality.

Unlike failed asset-heavy African agritech startups that collapsed under the weight of company-owned trucking fleets and inventory losses (e.g., Twiga Foods, iProcure), Zarati operates as an **asset-light, high-margin software coordination and intelligence layer**. 

**Core Revenue Engines:**
1. **B2G Sovereign SaaS Licenses:** Sold to the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry (MOAF) and State Ministries of Production for the National Agriculture Command Center, digital farmer registries, and harvest tax clearing.
2. **B2D Development & Humanitarian Contracts:** Annual data subscriptions and audit telemetry sold to international agencies (UN FAO, WFP, World Bank, IFAD) to track emergency input distribution and food balances.
3. **B2B Commodity Transaction Take-Rates:** A 1.0% to 2.5% software clearing fee on verified trade matches between commercial grain/oilseed traders, food processors (Savola, Almarai), and farmer cooperatives.
4. **Free Core Services for Farmers:** Fundamental agronomic advisory, spot price tickers, and weather risk alerts are delivered 100% free of charge to smallholder farmers, driving massive grassroots network effects.

## 2. The Problem: Structural Opacity in Emerging-Market Agriculture

Sudanese and regional agriculture operates in near-total informational darkness:
* **The Sovereign Data Void:** Physical land records, tenant files, and historical statistical archives across central Sudan were destroyed or displaced during the conflict. Government ministries cannot accurately quantify how many feddans were planted, which crops were harvested, or where regional famine deficits will strike.
* **Severe Market Price Asymmetry:** Smallholder farmers sell grains to local middlemen (*Kassaba*) at prices 30% to 50% below real market value due to complete lack of price visibility, while inflation and currency depreciation erode their working capital.
* **Physical Market Auction Inefficiencies:** Physical crop exchanges (Gedaref, El Obeid) operate on chalkboards and manual paper ledgers, causing cash-handling delays, municipal tax leakage, and opaque cross-border trade.
* **Humanitarian Aid Diversion & Verification Failures:** Multilateral donors distribute tens of millions of dollars in emergency seeds and fertilizers annually but lack an auditable, tamper-proof digital system to verify whether inputs reached intended smallholders.

## 3. The Zarati Solution: The Sovereign Agri-OS

Zarati delivers a unified, bilingual (Arabic-first) digital operating system comprising four integrated layers:
1. **The National Agriculture Command Center (`/intelligence`):** A high-density GIS decision-support portal for ministers, governors, and donor directors providing 5-tier drill-down from national cereal balances down to locality parcels.
2. **The Immutable Price Discovery Engine:** Multi-tier spot market feeds with real-time foreign exchange normalization (SDG $\leftrightarrow$ USD), giving all market actors certified closing prices with complete cryptographic provenance.
3. **The Verified B2B Marketplace & RFQ Engine:** An asset-light digital clearinghouse allowing verified commercial buyers (flour mills, exporters, GCC sovereign funds) to issue Requests for Quotations (RFQs) and contract grain lots directly from verified cooperatives with digital escrow settlement.
4. **The Low-Bandwidth Farmer Interface:** An offline-first PWA, SMS, and audio-assisted portal giving smallholders hyper-local weather alerts, actionable daily agronomic advice, and direct valuation of their crops.

## 6. Flagship Go-To-Market Plan: Gedaref State Zero

* **Phase 1 (Months 1–6):** Deploy in **Gedaref State** (the national granary). Onboard 5,000 smallholders, 150 commercial traders, and 10 agricultural cooperatives. Connect the Gedaref Crop Market physical auction to the digital Zarati price engine.
* **Phase 2 (Months 7–12):** Deploy the **Federal National Command Center** in Port Sudan in coordination with MOAF and the Agricultural Research Corporation (ARC).
* **Phase 3 (Year 2):** Expand to Kassala, River Nile, and Northern States; launch the Red Sea Export Provenance Corridor connecting Gedaref harvest to Saudi importers in Jeddah.
